<|det|>title [105, 83, 229, 98]<|/det|>4 Procedure
<|det|>text [104, 115, 877, 149]<|/det|>The procedure below was followed for every sample without exception. Departures from it were recorded on the sheet and there were four in the whole series, all of them in the first week.
<|det|>title [105, 173, 241, 189]<|/det|>4.1 Preparation
<|det|>text [131, 201, 837, 235]<|/det|>1. Weigh the wet sample to the nearest hundredth of a gram and record the mass on the sheet before anything is added to it.
<|det|>text [130, 246, 871, 280]<|/det|>2. Disaggregate in sodium hexametaphosphate for twelve hours. Do not stir; the tall jars are used for this reason and the short ones are not interchangeable with them.
<|det|>text [131, 291, 847, 325]<|/det|>3. Wet sieve at sixty three micrometres, retaining both fractions. The fine fraction goes to the settling column the same day.
<|det|>title [105, 352, 254, 367]<|/det|>4.2 Measurement
<|det|>text [130, 381, 825, 414]<|/det|>(a) Dry the coarse fraction at one hundred and five degrees to constant mass, then sieve at half phi intervals.
<|det|>text [130, 426, 880, 459]<|/det|>(b) Read the settling column at the eight standard times. The seventh reading is the one most often missed and it is the one that sets the tail of the distribution.
<|det|>text [130, 471, 884, 505]<|/det|>(c) Record both fractions on the same sheet. A sample split across two sheets has twice been reconstructed wrongly.
<|det|>text [104, 535, 894, 587]<|/det|>Duplicate measurements were made on one sample in twenty. The duplicate pairs agree to better than a tenth of a phi unit in the coarse fraction and to about a quarter in the fine, which is the usual asymmetry and is why the fine tail is not interpreted below the seventh reading.
