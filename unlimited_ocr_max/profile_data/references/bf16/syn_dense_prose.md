<|det|>title [105, 82, 327, 98]<|/det|>3 The sediment record
<|det|>text [104, 117, 894, 187]<|/det|>Sediment cores taken from the lower basin between 1962 and 1974 remain the only continuous record of deposition in the estuary before the barrage was closed. The cores were logged in the field at a resolution of one centimetre and subsampled at five, which is coarse by present standards but consistent throughout, and it is that consistency rather than the resolution which makes the series usable.
<|det|>text [104, 200, 871, 271]<|/det|>Two of the eleven cores were lost in storage. The remaining nine were rephotographed in 1998 under the original field lighting, and the photographs agree with the field logs everywhere except in the uppermost twenty centimetres of core six, where the log records a laminated grey silt that the photograph does not show. We treat that interval as unrecorded rather than as either observation.
<|det|>text [104, 285, 888, 356]<|/det|>Grain size was measured by sieve for the coarse fraction and by pipette below sixty three micrometres. The two methods overlap across a narrow band and disagree systematically within it, the pipette reading finer by roughly a third of a phi unit. No correction has been applied. Where a sample falls in the overlap the sieve value is quoted and the pipette value is given in the appendix.
<|det|>text [105, 367, 886, 440]<|/det|>The chronology rests on four radiocarbon dates and on the position of a single ash layer correlated with the 1947 eruption. The ash provides the only tie point in the upper half of the sequence and it is present in seven of the nine cores. In the two where it is absent the correlation is made on the sand horizon immediately beneath it, which is thicker and less easily mistaken but is not independently dated.
<|det|>text [104, 453, 875, 524]<|/det|>Accumulation rates derived from these tie points range from one point one to three point four millimetres per year, with the highest values in the cores nearest the northern channel. That gradient is the expected one and it is reproduced in every core pair we can compare directly. What is not expected is the reversal between 1968 and 1971, which appears in six cores and in none of the gauge records for the same interval.
<|det|>text [104, 538, 858, 608]<|/det|>We have not attempted to reconcile the two. The gauge record is a stage series and converting it to a sediment flux requires a rating curve that does not exist for those years. The reversal is reported here as it stands, with the note that it is the single feature of the series most likely to be an artefact of the correlation rather than of the estuary.
